Every friend group and every office has the same problem with the classic "guess who wrote it" game: someone has to collect the bits of paper, someone has to read them all out loud in a voice that gives nothing away, and it falls apart the second the group gets past about eight people.

[Who Said It](https://tryneegma.com/game/who-said-it) is that game with the paper, the handwriting, and the reader removed. Here's the exact screen the room is looking at once the answers are in.

## How Who Said It works

Who Said It is a host led game for 3 to 20 players, or 2 to 6 teams, playable online with everyone on their own phone or offline from a single shared device.

1. **One question goes to everyone at once.** The host either picks a prepared set of prompts built in the Playground, or types a question live on the spot and shares it there and then. Either way, there's no correct answer, just an honest one: things like "what's the most overrated food?" or "what's a small lie you tell all the time?"
2. **Everyone answers in private.** Every player types their own response on their own phone. The host sees a progress counter, not the answers themselves, so nothing leaks before the reveal.
3. **The answers come back with no names on them.** Once everyone's in, the board shows every answer shuffled and unsigned. By default they land as a word cloud, so if three people wrote the same thing, it visibly grows in the middle of the screen before anyone's even started guessing.
4. **The room guesses.** The host picks how: **All at Once** shows every answer together and each player pins all of them on a name in one go, which is the one to use for a bigger room. **One by One** reveals a single answer at a time for the group to argue over and vote on before moving to the next, which turns every answer into its own little scene, best kept to groups of about 8 or under.
5. **The authors are revealed, and the points land.** Scoring isn't a raw correct count, it's ranked: the best guesser in the room scores 1000, the weakest scorer among everyone who got at least one right scores 500, and anyone tied on the same number of correct guesses scores exactly the same. Guess nothing right and you score nothing, but you were never going to be caught out by an empty room either.

## Why Who Said It gets people talking about people, not trivia

### Nobody can hide behind a screen

Because every answer is graded on how well the room reads each other rather than general knowledge, the quietest person at the table has exactly the same shot at winning as the loudest. Half the fun is watching a room confidently pin the funniest answer on the wrong person entirely.

### The word cloud does the teasing for you

Answers default to a cloud rather than a flat list, so duplicate answers visibly swell as more people land on the same joke, the same complaint, or the same guilty pleasure. Nobody has to point it out. The room just sees it happen.

### Write your own questions, or make one up on the spot

There's no generic house question bank behind this one on purpose. Build a set in the Playground ahead of a hen do, an offsite, or a family dinner, or skip the prep entirely and type a question live when the room needs one. Both routes run through the same scoring, so a question you thought of thirty seconds ago counts exactly as much as one you planned for a week.

### Works from a dinner table to a full team offsite

Small groups can run it One by One and turn every answer into a proper discussion. Bigger rooms switch to All at Once and keep the same energy without the game dragging. Play as individuals or split into teams, who write and guess as one unit.

### Playable online or in the room

Cast the host screen to a TV or share it on a video call for remote players to answer from their own phones. In person with no phones out, pass a single device around the circle so everyone types in secret before handing it back. Same questions, same scoring, either way.

If you're after a game that turns a room of people who think they know each other into a room of people finding out they don't, Who Said It is worth an opening round.
