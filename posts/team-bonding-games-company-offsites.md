The awkward part of a company offsite is rarely the strategy session. It's the ten minutes before it starts, when people arrive in small clusters, scan the room for a familiar face, make a drink, and wait for someone else to kick off a conversation.

A good team activity fixes that without putting anyone on the spot. It gives people a small shared challenge, a reason to laugh, and a low pressure way to interact with colleagues they don't usually work alongside. Here are 15 team bonding games that work at real offsites, plus a simple way to build them into a day for groups of 10, 25, 50, or 100.

## First, choose the right kind of game

Before picking an activity, work out what the room actually needs.

- **New or cross-functional team:** conversation led games that make introductions easy.
- **A group that needs waking up:** short, competitive rounds with a timer.
- **A large room:** put people into teams by table, department, or colour, so everyone has people to play alongside.
- **An evening social:** more playful games that invite performance, bluffing, or friendly debate.

The goal isn't to force bonding. It's to create enough shared moments that the rest of the offsite feels warmer and more natural.

## 15 offsite games that work

### 1. Two Truths & a Lie

Each person shares three statements about themselves, and the group tries to spot the lie in [Two Truths & a Lie](https://tryneegma.com/game/two-truths-and-a-lie). It's an easy first activity because nobody needs specialist knowledge or physical confidence to join in.

**Best for:** morning arrivals, new teams, groups of 4 to 15.

### 2. Social Bingo

Build a card with prompts like "has lived in more than two countries" or "has an unusual commute" using [Social Bingo](https://tryneegma.com/game/bingo). Players have to actually go and meet people in the room to complete their squares.

**Best for:** networking-style arrivals, groups of 20 to 100.

### 3. Find the Mole

Most players get the same category and secret word. One person, the mole, only gets the category. Everyone gives clues, then votes on who seems not to know the word, in [Find the Mole](https://tryneegma.com/game/find-the-mole).

It works because everyone has to listen closely and build a case. The discussion often keeps going well after the round ends.

**Best for:** teams that enjoy a bit of social deduction, groups of 4 to 20.

### 4. Team Taboo

A player describes a secret word without using the banned terms on the card, while their team races the clock to guess it, in [Taboo](https://tryneegma.com/game/taboo).

**Best for:** energising a room after lunch, groups of 10 to 100 in teams.

### 5. Charades

The classic still works, especially once one brave person is willing to go first. Give teams a prompt, set a short timer, and let them act and shout their way to the answer with [Charades](https://tryneegma.com/game/charades).

**Best for:** evening socials and confident groups, 10 to 100 in teams.

### 6. Custom Unscramble

Players race to solve scrambled words or phrases with [Unscramble](https://tryneegma.com/game/unscramble). Use company terms sparingly, mixed with fun questions about the team, the city, the industry, or shared cultural references.

**Best for:** a fast opener or a five minute reset, 10 to 100 players.

### 7. Missing Letter

Show a word or phrase with letters removed and let players fill the gaps with [Missing Letter](https://tryneegma.com/game/missing-letter). It's simple enough that people get it immediately, but a timer makes every round surprisingly competitive.

**Best for:** a low explanation group game, 10 to 100 players.

### 8. Word Art

A criterion appears, something like "a word you'd use to describe a great manager." Players or teams have seconds to give an answer that fits, and the room decides whether it counts, with [Word Art](https://tryneegma.com/game/word-art).

**Best for:** quick thinking and laughter, teams that like to challenge each other, 4 to 50 players.

### 9. Song Association

Give the group a word. The first player or team has to sing a lyric containing it before time runs out, with [Song Association](https://tryneegma.com/game/song-association). It's less about vocal talent and more about the chaos of everyone remembering a different song at once.

**Best for:** an informal end of day session, 4 to 50 players.

### 10. The company timeline

Give tables major moments from the company's history, products, or team milestones, and have them put them in the right order. Add a few harmless decoys to keep it competitive.

**Best for:** milestone celebrations and all-hands offsites, 15 to 100 players.

### 11. The photo challenge

Give teams five to ten prompts to capture around the venue: a group photo recreating an album cover, something that represents a company value, or the most dramatic coffee order in the building.

**Best for:** venue based offsites and groups who need to move around, 10 to 50 players.

### 12. What do we have in common?

Split into small groups and challenge them to find five things everyone shares that aren't work related. The oddest valid commonality earns bonus points.

**Best for:** new teams and retreats, 8 to 40 players.

### 13. The pitch challenge

Give each team an ordinary object and five minutes to pitch it as a ridiculous new product. The room votes for the most convincing, or funniest, presentation.

**Best for:** creative teams and afternoon sessions, 10 to 60 players.

### 14. Office awards, anonymously

Collect light prompts in advance: most likely to have a survival plan, best at finding a discount, most likely to reply before 8am. Keep it kind, opt in, and well away from anything personal.

**Best for:** teams that already know each other, 10 to 50 players.

### 15. Let every game crown its own winner

Instead of trying to hold one running score across a whole afternoon of different games, let each short game have its own leaderboard and its own winner. It's genuinely more fun this way, because a team that had a quiet Unscramble round can still take the Charades trophy twenty minutes later, and the room gets several moments to cheer instead of pinning everything on one final tally.

**Best for:** any offsite with 15 to 100 players.

## A simple 45 minute offsite game plan

You don't need an entire afternoon of activities. A well paced 45 minutes can carry the energy of the day.

| Time             | Activity                           | Purpose                                    |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 0 to 10 minutes  | Social Bingo or Two Truths & a Lie | Help people arrive and start conversations |
| 10 to 20 minutes | Custom Unscramble                  | An early, low stakes competitive win       |
| 20 to 35 minutes | Taboo or Charades in teams         | Build shared energy at each table          |
| 35 to 42 minutes | Find the Mole or Word Art          | A change of pace for the group             |
| 42 to 45 minutes | Leaderboard and a small prize      | Finish on a memorable moment               |
