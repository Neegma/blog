A corporate event can have the right venue, excellent food, a thoughtful speaker lineup, and a polished run of show, and people will still end up standing in the same familiar circles, checking their phones between agenda items.

The problem isn't that people dislike the event. It's that most events ask guests to be spectators for too long.

A well chosen game changes the role of the audience. Instead of waiting for the next speaker, people have something to solve, debate, sing, act out, or celebrate together, and the room turns social before the networking portion has even officially started. Here are 12 corporate event games that work for offsites, award nights, client events, brand activations, and team socials.

## Match the game to the moment

The best activity depends on what the room needs right then. Use this before you plan the finer details.

| Event moment             | The job                                     | Best game types                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Arrival and registration | Give strangers an easy reason to talk       | Social Bingo, Two Truths & a Lie          |
| Before a keynote         | Focus the room without eating much time     | Unscramble, Missing Letter                |
| Post lunch energy dip    | Wake everyone up together                   | Taboo, Word Art, Song Association         |
| Networking break         | Mix people across familiar groups           | Bingo, team challenges                    |
| Evening social           | Create louder, more memorable moments       | Charades, Find the Mole, Song Association |
| Finale or prize moment   | Build anticipation and reward participation | A live leaderboard reveal                 |

## 12 games for corporate events

### 1. Social Bingo

[Social Bingo](https://tryneegma.com/game/bingo) is built for mixers. Instead of only marking numbers, attendees find people who match prompts like "has worked in three industries" or "can recommend a great local restaurant." It's one of the least awkward ways to get a room talking, because the game hands everyone an opening line.

**Best for:** registration, welcome drinks, and networking breaks.

### 2. Custom Unscramble

Show a scrambled word on the main screen and give players a short countdown to solve it from their phones with [Unscramble](https://tryneegma.com/game/unscramble). Build a set around the event theme, the company, the destination, or a product launch.

**Best for:** fast conference transitions, groups of 20 to 100.

### 3. Missing Letter

A word or phrase appears with letters missing. Players submit the completed answer before time runs out with [Missing Letter](https://tryneegma.com/game/missing-letter). It's simple, accessible, and works well as a two minute reset between formal sessions.

**Best for:** mixed age or mixed seniority groups, 20 to 100 players.

### 4. Two Truths & a Lie

This classic becomes more useful shaped around professional life: a surprising past job, an unexpected skill, a memorable work trip. Use a few volunteers rather than going round the whole room, in [Two Truths & a Lie](https://tryneegma.com/game/two-truths-and-a-lie).

**Best for:** smaller team socials and leadership events.

### 5. Team Taboo

Teams describe a word while avoiding a short list of forbidden ones in [Taboo](https://tryneegma.com/game/taboo). It's fast, loud, and lands perfectly after lunch, when the room needs to react rather than sit and listen. Switch on Team Mode and let each table pick and name its own team as they join, and you skip the awkward "who's on whose side" conversation entirely.

**Best for:** team socials and large company events, 30 to 100 people.

### 6. Charades

[Charades](https://tryneegma.com/game/charades) works exactly as well corporately as it does at home. A performer acts out a word or phrase while their team guesses. If someone doesn't fancy being first up, any teammate can jump in and take the turn instead, so nobody's stuck waiting on a reluctant volunteer.

**Best for:** evening socials and awards dinners.

### 7. Word Art

In [Word Art](https://tryneegma.com/game/word-art), a creative criterion appears and the active player or team has to offer a fitting answer before the clock runs out. The room gets to decide whether it counts, which turns even a simple answer into a proper debate.

**Best for:** energetic small groups or table versus table competition.

### 8. Find the Mole

[Find the Mole](https://tryneegma.com/game/find-the-mole) asks players to work out who's only been given a category rather than the actual secret word. It generates discussion quickly and lands especially well once a group has had its fill of straightforward trivia.

**Best for:** networking dinners, team socials, groups of 4 to 20.

### 9. Song Association

Give a player or team a word, and they have to sing a lyric that contains it, in [Song Association](https://tryneegma.com/game/song-association). It has an unusual power to turn a polite room into a genuinely loud one, so save it for when the event's already gone informal.

**Best for:** after work socials and award night entertainment.

### 10. The company timeline challenge

Put teams in charge of arranging company, product, or industry moments into the right order. Add a few entertaining decoys and use it to celebrate progress without the session feeling like a slide deck in disguise.

**Best for:** anniversaries, company milestones, all-hands events.

### 11. The client or product quiz

Use a custom question set to highlight a product, campaign, client story, or event theme, built through the Playground. Keep it audience first: interesting facts and playful misconceptions land better than a run of sales messages disguised as trivia.

**Best for:** brand activations, partner events, product launches.

### 12. Let each game crown its own winner

Rather than trying to run one long score across an entire evening, let every short game have its own leaderboard and its own moment on screen. A team that had a quiet Unscramble round can still walk away with the Find the Mole trophy twenty minutes later, and the room gets several genuine celebration moments instead of pinning everything on one final tally.

**Best for:** any corporate event with prizes, an MC, or an evening programme.

## A 20 minute live game show run for 50 to 100 people

For a larger room, keep it simple. This works between sessions, after lunch, or right before the evening programme starts.

1. **Minutes 0 to 3: join.** Put a QR code on the main screen and invite guests to join from their phones.
2. **Minutes 3 to 8: custom Unscramble.** Run five quick event themed words to get the room playing immediately.
3. **Minutes 8 to 15: Team Taboo or Word Art.** Split guests by table, department, or coloured wristband. Give each team a shot at the points.
4. **Minutes 15 to 18: a final quick fire round.** Missing Letter, or a simple themed question set.
5. **Minutes 18 to 20: reveal the scoreboard.** Celebrate the top team and hand straight back to the event programme.
