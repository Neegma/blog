Every conference organiser knows the dreaded mid afternoon slump.

After two back to back panel discussions, a slide heavy keynote, and a buffet lunch, the energy in the room drops off a cliff. Attendees start checking laptops, scrolling email, or quietly slipping out for another coffee they don't really need.

You don't fix that with another passive Q&A. You need a fast, genuinely interactive five to ten minute reset that gets the whole room laughing, thinking, and talking to the person next to them. Here's how facilitators and organisers are using [Neegma](https://tryneegma.com/use-cases/conferences) to turn transitions into the moment people actually remember from the day.

## Why the usual conference icebreakers fall flat

Most audience engagement tactics at larger events fail for the same handful of reasons.

- **App fatigue.** Ask 200 attendees to download a dedicated conference app for one mini game, and most of them won't bother.
- **Forced networking.** "Turn to your neighbour and share an uncomfortable fact" creates tension, not fun.
- **Boring polling widgets.** A standard multiple choice poll feels like an extension of the last slide deck, not a break from it.

## The Neegma approach: ten minutes of real game show energy

Neegma turns audience engagement into a host led live game running straight on your auditorium screen, controlled from the facilitator's own laptop or phone.

### 1. Instant join, no app required

Between sessions, the MC or facilitator puts the Neegma QR code up on the main projector. Attendees point their phone camera at it and they're in the lobby in seconds, straight in their browser. No download, no account, no login screen standing between them and playing.

### 2. Custom, industry specific content

Using the [Neegma Playground](https://tryneegma.com/features), organisers can build question decks in advance instead of relying on generic pop culture trivia. Questions can celebrate company milestones, poke fun at industry inside jokes, or test knowledge tied to the day's themes, using games like:

- **[Unscramble](https://tryneegma.com/game/unscramble):** rearranging scrambled industry terminology against a live countdown.
- **[Missing Letter](https://tryneegma.com/game/missing-letter):** rapid fire completion of technical terms or speaker quotes.
- **[Find the Mole](https://tryneegma.com/game/find-the-mole):** a quick social deduction round that gets entire rows of seats whispering and debating.

If your audience is naturally seated in rows, blocks, or tables, switching on Team Mode and letting each group pick and name its own team turns a passive audience into a room full of small rivalries before you've even finished explaining the rules.

### 3. A leaderboard the room can actually see

As answers roll in, the leaderboard on the main screen reshuffles live. That shift from passive listening to active competition wakes a room up instantly, sparks rivalries between seating sections, and gives the MC natural material to riff on between rounds.

### 4. Straight back into the agenda

Because the host controls exactly how many rounds run, a full icebreaker can wrap in as little as six to eight minutes. When it ends, the top scorer is shown on screen, the room applauds, and everyone's primed and attentive for the next speaker.
